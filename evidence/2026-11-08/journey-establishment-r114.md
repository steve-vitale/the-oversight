## Village assessment (plain words)

Sixteen villages, six weeks in. Every one is still standing, which is the first
thing to say — nobody starved, nobody scattered. The pantries are mostly fat
(only 17105 is tight at 6,516 kcal, exactly where it was before the change). The
killing is uneven as always: 17110's hunters brought down 63 beasts while 17105,
17107 and 17111 brought down none — but the no-kill villages aren't idle, they're
cooking: 17111's cooks ran the racks 89 times. The change did what it was meant
to: the people doing the preserving are now visibly getting better at it, and in
five villages that had never produced a new preserver before, someone learned the
craft for the first time. The cost is speed — a few cooks raced from knowing
nothing to being the village's best preserver in six weeks, which is faster than
the craft should probably travel. And the old pattern hasn't gone away: in two
villages the same few hands still soak up all the learning, and in one village
the learners keep dying before anyone can count them.
