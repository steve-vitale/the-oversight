## Reproduction note (read before the table)

The 4 seeds did **not** reproduce on current master (26ce0f51): kill counts, end
days, and end reasons all diverged. An `ATTR_OFF=1` control (instrumentation
fully gated via `Game._attrKills=false`) diverged identically, proving the
instrumentation is not the cause. Root cause: four game-logic commits landed
between the evidence run's base and 26ce0f51 (killer's tale-telling 4d9c925e,
moot quorum bbb75d54, promisedecay 48400370, journey endgame r9 a0e73240).

The attribution below was therefore produced on the evidence era's exact code
(ad76cd8a — the telemetry suite's own commit, which includes the villager-counter
arm e79a08de) with the attribution instrumentation ported onto it. All four runs
reproduce the evidence exactly: same end day, same end reason, same wave-3
ledger kill counts (1, 1, 1, 3). The instrumentation does not perturb trajectories.

## Finding

**0 of 6 wave-3 kills involved trick execution.** Five of six kills came through
the player's own turn-based combat, not autonomous villager field fights — the
pilot's "villager kill" classification was by assumption ("the player doesn't
hunt on courtship policies"), and the channel tags disprove it: the player does
fight wave-3 monsters in TB when they come. The single genuine villager kill
(chloe_dubois vs redactor, wild encounter, day 71) executed no trick in that
fight. Trick executions did occur in 3 of the 4 runs (14 total: shouts, stills,
one spool-record wait), but in fights that produced no wave-3 kills.

The villager-counter arm's GO verdict ("4 villager wave-3 kills vs 0/16 pre-arm")
does not survive attribution: the kills were player kills, and the new
trick-execution paths caused none of them. The arm's real, measured effect is
the trick executions themselves (villagers do learn and execute counters —
14 executions across 4 runs, zero pre-arm by construction) plus the median
survival parity (73 vs 71). Whether trick executions convert into kills at a
higher rate than baseline fighting remains unmeasured — that is the next
experiment, not this one.
