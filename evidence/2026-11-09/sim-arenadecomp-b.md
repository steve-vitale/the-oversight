**No death-reduction lever follows**, for three measured reasons (not a shrug — each is a number):
1. **Share:** 9.9% is a tenth of pre-floor deaths; the binding killer is the wild at 87.1%. The winnability chain's binding links are elsewhere.
2. **Mechanism:** the deaths are canon-correct. Blood contests are MEANT to kill (CONTESTS.md: "Death is on the table"); these are fair long fights — median 62.5 rounds — against wave-appropriate beasts, entered at full HP (median entry 100), by scholars who are not veterans. There is no unfairness to fix, no already-hurt victim pool, no veteran drain.
3. **Choice:** the choice channel is not the problem. 7 of 10 were grabs by design ("usually it grabs you"), and all 3 who were offered a choice chose to participate after being told the risk (one was told "EXTREME RISK — almost nobody walks away from this one" and walked in anyway). The choice worked where it existed.

**The one named follow-up is an honesty fix, not a death lever:** the honest-stakes ledger (`contestDeathsById`) never counts arena deaths — it reads `{}` on all 8 seeds after 10 arena deaths — so the choice screen tells the next scholar "No one has died in this one yet" a week after the same contest killed the last scholar in the same village (proven live: Sal, d28 — see Sense test). The fix is to count arena deaths in the ledger, so the "This one has killed N before" line tells the truth. It changes the CHOICE, not the death rate. (Finding (b).)

Notes on the join (read before reusing it):
- **Entry HP is the take-day HP, not the death-day dawn HP.** The death-day dawn snapshot does not contain the victim — they are already taken (e.g. 43001: roster pop 11 on d33 → 9 on d34, Judith absent from the health map), so the join reads the last row containing them (the take-day row, labeled per row in the analyzer output). - **The hurt trail is empty on all 10 death days — and that is the finding, not a gap.** 1,063 hurtVillager events were recorded band-wide, but none falls on an arena victim's death day: the arena fight's damage lives in the tactical-fighter object (`_taken` 95–105), never passes through `hurtVillager`, and the scholar enters at ~100 HP and dies inside the fight. A villager fighter dying inside an arena fight would settle via `hurtVillager(..., 'contest')` and carry cause `contest`, not `the arena` — consistent with 0 villager arena deaths.

## Village assessment — what the band's villages looked like (plain words)

Eight villages, seven lost, one still standing at the 150-day horizon. They are the same deed-seeking villages the prefloor band buried — the scholar walks out to fight, usually alone, and usually dies in the wild (88 of 101 pre-floor deaths). The arena takes another 10, and the shape of those 10 is now plain: it is not a plague or a famine or a veteran drain. It is the show coming for the leader, about once per village, in the first two months — a summons, a grab or a choice, a long fight in front of a crowd against a beast the village has already met in the wild, and a grave by the fire. The villages do not collapse from it; they absorb it the way they absorb everything else, one succession at a time. The cruelest seed, 43004, buried three scholars in the arena (Grace, Karl, Diana) and still lasted 80 days. The longest-lived, 43006, survived to the horizon with no arena death at all — the arena never took it, and the wild still kept it at regional, never national. The arena is a tenth of the dying, and it kills the way the show is supposed to kill: in public, after a fair fight, with the village watching. What is wrong is not the dying. What is wrong is the record: the show tells the next scholar nobody has died in the contest that killed the last one.

**43001, day 34 — Judith dies in The Pit (speedbump_turtle, wave 1, 9 rounds).** The lines land as a sequence, and they cohere: "📺 The gate slams up. Nothing reads robotic; what reads wrong is the *matchup*: a wave-1 turtle killing a full-health scholar in the arena is the design working as written (the beast is wave-appropriate, the fight is real), and the fiction knows it is a tragedy, not a bug.

**43003, day 37 — Brian dies in the Siege (nightcourt, wave 1, 156 rounds).** He was OFFERED the choice, and the choice screen told him the truth about the risk and a lie about the record: "📺 The billing says EXTREME RISK — almost nobody walks away from this one. / 📺 No one has died in this one yet. ## Metric review

- **Metrics held.** The primary metric (arena share of pre-90 deaths + full classification of every arena death) was judged exactly as pre-registered: 10/101 = 9.9%, 10/10 classified, 0 unclassified, raw census printed, 0 missing days. No friendly re-scoring anywhere in this verdict. The subset (9/85 = 10.6%) agrees with the primary set, so no judgment call was needed between them.
- **Prereg nuance correction (mechanism description, not a metric change):** the prereg says "no choice-taken flag exists on it (`ac._refused` is set only on refusal)". That is incomplete: `contestChoose` records every phase choice in `ac._approach[phaseIdx]` (contests.js:4361), so when a choice phase exists (phaseIdx 0), `approach[0]` IS the participate/refuse choice. The join uses it ONLY when the take offered a choice (otherwise `approach[0]` is the first real phase's choice — fixture E in the proof guards exactly this). No bar or number changes: the count that matters (offered 3, all participated) is read from the take + approach together, and the verdict does not depend on the correction. The mandatory/voluntary split as a *stored engine fact* remains unrecorded — what this arm adds is the observed say-line signal plus the approach record, both engine-exposed.
- **Instrument correction (before any judged number survived):** the first instrument build captured the choice-offer line via the say-wrap only and measured 0 offers on 55 takes. That zero was an INSTRUMENT ARTIFACT, not a game finding: `scripts/sim-harness.js` silences BOTH `G.say` and `G.sysSay` with no-ops, and `sysSay` is a separate function that does NOT route through `say` — so the choice-offer line (said via `this.sysSay`, contests.js:2268) and every contest death line never reached the say-wrap. The fix wraps `G.sysSay` call-through (to the harness no-op): System lines now land in the say window, the death sample, and the choice-offer count. Pure observation — no RNG, no Game-state writes. The 6 seeds run under the first build were discarded (recorded corpora deleted, summary never written) and the FULL band re-run under the fixed instrument; the smoke seed re-run under the fixed instrument produced a byte-identical fingerprint (same days, same deaths), confirming neutrality. No judged number in this file comes from the broken build. **Standing lesson for the next arm (finding (c)):** any recorder that only wraps `say()` is deaf to every System line.
- **The 50% bar did its job by not applying.** The arena share (9.9%) is far below the wild's 87.1% — the arm's job was decomposition, not dominance, and the bar for it was classification completeness (met 10/10), not a share threshold.
- **sysSayLines cap:** the lines array caps at 400 per run (43008 hit it); it is a sample on long runs, while the choice-offer COUNT is uncapped and exact. A future arm needing full System transcripts should raise the cap or count-only.
- **Day-gap note (instrument, not a metric failure):** daily snapshots are missing on a few days per seed (day 4 on most seeds; 43001 also day 45) because the clock can advance >1 day per policy.daily call (multi-day travel/legs). The G3 length rule (daySeries == daysAlive or daysAlive-1) held on all 8 seeds; the gaps are recorded as notes naming the day, per the feveranat G3 precedent. No arena death falls on a gap day for its entry-HP read (entry HP falls back to the take-day row, labeled).
