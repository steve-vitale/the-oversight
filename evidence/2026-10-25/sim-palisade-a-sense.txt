SENSE JUDGMENT (meaning, not counts):
- The beat reads as escalation, not noise: it lands immediately after
  the RAID line, before the call-for-help line, in the same voice as
  the raid announcement; "the wall was not built for its kind" says
  the palisade itself is outmatched, which is the escalation the old
  format could not express. The day-60 contrast proves the negative:
  the same village, the same wall, a wave-2 apex — and silence,
  correctly.
- Fog-of-war / knowledge gating: the beat never names a wave number
  or wave title, and it uses exactly the RAID line's display-name
  convention (mname / monsterDisplayName). Late in this replay the
  names shown are true names (Buffering, Spool) — but that is the
  RAID line's own convention once the System has arrived (the RAID
  line itself prints those names); the beat leaks nothing the RAID
  line does not already print. Early-game, before the System arrives,
  the proof variants show the gated form ("a tangle of tape that
  moves like it's remembering", "a missing piece of the world").
- One honest wrinkle, judged acceptable: on a raid with two wave-3+
  raiders (day 161) the beat names only the first. "One of them"
  keeps it honest — it never claims to be a roster — and naming both
  would turn an alarm beat into a census. The escalation signal is
  "something bigger came", not "here is the inventory".
